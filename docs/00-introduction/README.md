# Introduction

Welcome to the **AEM Technical Lead Playbook**: a practical, engineering-first learning resource for Adobe Experience Manager (AEM) developers, senior engineers, technical leads, and future solution architects.

The goal is not to memorize APIs or collect disconnected tutorials. It is to build the judgment to understand how systems behave, investigate production evidence, evaluate trade-offs, and communicate sound technical decisions.

## Who this is for

- **AEM developers** strengthening their understanding of platform internals and implementation.
- **Senior engineers** growing their ownership of performance, reliability, security, and integrations.
- **Technical leads** guiding design decisions, code quality, delivery, and team learning.
- **Future solution architects** connecting business context to cross-system architecture and governance.

You do not need to read every chapter in order. Choose the path that matches your current responsibilities, then return to the shared foundations when a topic exposes a gap.

## Start your journey

1. **Understand the engineering mindset.** Read [Engineering Principles](../00-engineering-principles/README.md) to learn the decision-making standards used throughout the playbook.
2. **Choose a route.** Follow the [Learning Roadmap](../01-learning-roadmap/README.md) for role-oriented sequences and practical checkpoints.
3. **Study AEM request behavior.** Begin with [Understanding AEM Internals](../20-understanding-aem-internals/README.md), which follows a request from the delivery edge through Sling, resource/script resolution, rendering, and the repository.
4. **Practice with evidence.** Use the available [engineering checklists](../12-checklists/README.md), templates, and production scenarios when relevant.
5. **Track what is available.** Consult the [Release Dashboard](../release-dashboard.md) and [Roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) to distinguish published material from planned work.

## How to use a chapter

For each topic:

- Identify the system behavior or engineering outcome you need to understand.
- Follow the request, data, configuration, or failure path rather than jumping straight to a fix.
- Write down assumptions and the evidence that would confirm or reject them.
- Consider security, performance, reliability, operability, and maintainability.
- Validate conclusions against the AEM version, deployment model, and environment you actually use.

Examples and guidance are learning aids, not substitutes for environment-specific verification or current Adobe documentation.

## Learning philosophy

The playbook prioritizes engineering judgment before feature lists. It encourages simple and maintainable designs, explicit trade-offs, production evidence, secure defaults, and continuous learning from reviews and incidents.
